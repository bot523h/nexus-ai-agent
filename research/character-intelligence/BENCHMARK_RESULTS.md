## Reading this honestly

**What the 12/12 means.** Each scenario declares an acceptable set of outcomes
*before* the run, and the engine produced one of them. The look-alike was rejected
(face distance 0.449 vs reject 0.215) and the canon contradiction was named as a
canon contradiction, not as drift. Those are the two scenarios that test the
engine's judgement rather than its bookkeeping.

**What the 12/12 does not mean.** The scenario frames are not a hard sweep:

* five genuine scenarios sit at face distance 0.000 because an eval frame and a
  gallery frame are near-identical renders — trivially verified;
* the face dimension's *held-out* operating point is FAR **0.600** / FRR **0.250**
  on 4 genuine and 10 impostor scores. On a harder sweep the same boundary would
  raise many false alarms. The scenario pass rate is therefore **not** an accuracy
  claim;
* voice and motion have **one** impostor sample each; their EER of 0.0 is
  meaningless and is reported only to show the pipeline runs;
* hair has **no** calibrated threshold (no hair-change samples exist in the
  corpus) and is advisory by decision;
* the corpus is procedural; nothing here transfers to photographs
  (`NOT_MEASURED`).

**The one result that is genuinely reassuring** is the separation structure: face
drift variants (0.30–0.68) and the distinct character (0.84–0.90) sit well above
same-identity conditions (0.00–0.04 except the clipped lighting frame), and
wardrobe same/different outfits separate with EER 0.0 on held-out pairs (n=3/3).
The *ordering* is right; the *margin* is thin.

`shot-light` is pruned from calibration because its face quality falls below the
gate (clipping under a 3400 K key at 1.18 intensity); `shot-environment` frames are
pruned because a canon-declared scar makes the difference *expected* and letting
them widen the identity band would weaken every later comparison.
