## Five options, and why prepend won

1. Edit `handlers.py` and delete the stubs. Rejected: live foreign lease.
2. Wait for the lease TTL. Rejected: the commands would stay simulated until then.
3. Register real handlers in unleased `bot/app.py` before `build_handlers`.
   Chosen. Smallest change that makes the commands true without touching the fence.
4. Mutate the handler list after `build_handlers` returns. Rejected: fragile,
   and it still depends on private list order.
5. A second bot process. Rejected: two pollers, no new consistency, too large.

`build_handlers` still exposes `post`, `schedule`, `ban`, `unban`, `stats`,
`welcome`, and `pin`. Those names are a contract of `tests/unit/test_handlers.py`.
The prepend does not remove them.

## Behaviour that shipped

- Ads: owner-only create/list/pause/resume/delete/stats. Validation before write.
  Cap of 20 active-or-paused campaigns per chat. Delivery claims the row
  (`next_run` advanced, or status `completed`) before `send_message`. Unbound
  bot claims nothing. One in-process loop, started and stopped beside the
  reminder lifecycle, in its own `try`. Not multi-process exactly-once.
- Channel: the seven commands perform the real operation or return a validation
  error. `/post` targets the current chat, not the hardcoded nightly channel id.
  Schedules persist. `restore_pending` reschedules `pending` only. A row moves
  to `sending` before send, then `sent` or `failed`. Shutdown cancels tasks
  that have not yet claimed. `{name}` is replaced literally; other braces are
  not evaluated. No stored welcome keeps the generic `Welcome {name} to the group!`.
- Onboarding: first successful `/start` sends the existing locale keyboard and
  then persists `UserLanguage`. A failed send does not persist. Missing
  `language_code` stores `en`. An existing row is not overwritten and does not
  get the keyboard again. Referral welcome and the silent self-referral path
  are unchanged.

Owner id `0` (the settings default) is not an operator. Non-owner calls do not
write and do not call Telegram.

## Adversarial notes

Tried: non-owner, unset owner id, empty and non-numeric input, past and
far-future schedule times, concurrent `claim_due`, send failure after claim,
restart of an overdue schedule, shutdown of a future schedule, missing
language, failed onboarding send, unknown callback data, self-referral, and
the existing `/start` referral tests.

Residual risk: a crash after claim and before a successful send skips that
interval or that scheduled post. That is the at-most-once choice. A second
process running the same database could still double-send; this repository's
bot is one process, and a distributed lease was not invented to paper over that.
